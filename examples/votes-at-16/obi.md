# 16-year-olds should be allowed to vote in general elections. {slug: votes-at-16, by: Ms. Smith}

- [+] Sixteen-year-olds can work and pay tax, so they should have a say in how it is spent. {slug: pay-tax, weight: 80, by: Sam}
  - [-] Most 16-year-olds don't earn enough to pay income tax. {slug: little-income-tax, weight: 30, by: Alex}
  - [+] They still pay VAT on everything they buy. {slug: pay-vat, weight: 60, by: Obi}
- [+] Decisions made now affect young people for longer than anyone else. {slug: longest-affected, weight: 70, by: Billie}
- [+] Schools already teach citizenship, so 16-year-olds are prepared to vote. {slug: citizenship-classes, weight: 55, by: Obi}
- [+] Turnout among young voters is low, and voting at 16 could help raise it. {slug: low-turnout, weight: 40, by: Billie}
- [-] Teenagers don't have enough experience to vote responsibly. {slug: inexperience, weight: 40, by: Li}
  - [+] Most 16-year-olds don't have jobs or haven't done any paid work. {slug: no-jobs, weight: 20, by: Obi}
  - [+] Teenagers' lives are mostly controlled by their parents so a lot of us don't really get to experience independence. {slug: parental-control, weight: 30, by: Li}
  - [+] Many 16-year-olds would simply vote the way their parents do. {slug: copy-parents, weight: 45, by: Alex}
  - [-] A lot of adults have pretty limited life experiences too. {slug: adults-limited-too, weight: 80, by: Alex}
  - [-] Many teenagers have had life experiences that help them understand how politics affects their lives. {slug: relevant-experiences, weight: 70, by: Billie}
    - [+] Young people are directly affected by policies on schools, transport and housing. {slug: affected-by-policy, weight: 60, by: Obi}
- [-] Most other adult rights, like buying alcohol, start at 18. {slug: adult-rights-at-18, weight: 35, by: Sam}
