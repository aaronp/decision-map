# The thesis {slug: thesis, by: Name}

- [+] A pro {slug: a-pro, weight: 60, confidence: 4}
  - [basis] What would change my mind about it
  - [ref] [Source name](https://example.org) — optional note
  - [ref] A source without a link
  - [-] A con of that pro {slug: its-con, weight: 40, by: Name}
- [-] A con of the thesis {slug: a-con, weight: 70}

# Or a proposition with premises {slug: which-way}

- [*] First premise {slug: premise-one}
  - [+] Why it holds {slug: why-one, weight: 60}
- [*] Second premise {slug: premise-two}

Two spaces per level. [+] supports its parent, [-] opposes it. A root can instead hold premises, [*] with no indent and no weight, each with its own pros and cons; a root holds either premises or pros/cons, not both. A premise is sized by its rolled-up strength. A pro or con may also carry confidence: 1–5 and one [basis] line (what would change your mind). Evidence is a [ref] line one level under a pro or con: a name (as a markdown link when there is a URL), then optionally “ — ” and a note. Every line needs a unique slug (the identity used by Diff) and a weight from 0 to 100; by is optional. Strength rolls up: a claim holds at (1 + Σ pro) ÷ (1 + Σ pro + Σ con), where each child counts as weight ÷ 100 × its own strength.
